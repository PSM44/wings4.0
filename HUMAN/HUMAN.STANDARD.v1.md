# HUMAN Standard v1 — Initial Foundation

## Definition

HUMAN is the canonical pedagogical layer through which a person understands, uses, evaluates, and governs a portfolio, project, product, system, or module.

Audience order:
1. Human
2. AI
3. Technical operators and developers

## Mandatory questions

A conforming HUMAN must explain:
- what the subject is;
- why it exists;
- who uses it;
- how a human interacts with it;
- what it owns and does not own;
- what decisions remain human;
- how AI participates and is constrained;
- its dependencies and portfolio relationships;
- its success criteria, risks, assumptions, and limitations.

## Canonicality rules

- Narrative sections hold the current canonical explanation.
- Q&A preserves accumulated questions and answer provenance.
- HUMAN.AUDIT evaluates semantic, pedagogical, and doctrinal quality.
- Dynamic execution state belongs in BATON, not HUMAN.
- Machine-generated inventory belongs in RADAR, not HUMAN.
- WHOAMI is not mandatory canon; stable identity content belongs in HUMAN.

## Growth rule

Use a single HUMAN file for small subjects. Create a HUMAN folder only when a real semantic boundary and sufficient content justify it.
